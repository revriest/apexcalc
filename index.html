How 3D Print Pricing & Cost Estimation Works

Calculating the true commercial cost of a 3D print requires factoring in far more than just raw filament weight. Many hobbyists and small print farms undercharge by ignoring electricity, machine wear, print failure risks, and setup labor.

Total Commercial Price = (Filament Cost + Electricity + Machine Wear + Labor) × (1 + Failure Risk %) × (1 + Markup %)

The Key Cost Components Explained:

  • Filament Cost: Calculated per gram using the total cost of your spool divided by its net weight (e.g., $25 / 1000g = $0.025/g).
  • Electricity Consumption: Based on printer power consumption in Watts during continuous printing. A typical FDM printer uses 100W–200W, whereas heated bed spikes occur primarily during warm-up.
  • Machine Wear & Maintenance: Covers replacement nozzles, PTFE tubes, PEI build sheets, stepper motors, and belt replacements over time (typically $0.25–$0.75 per operating hour).
  • Labor & Post-Processing: Slicing, bed levelling, support removal, sanding, and packing time calculated at your hourly labor rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What markup percentage should I charge for 3D printing services?
Standard commercial print farms generally apply a 50% to 150% markup for standard production parts. Custom prototyping, engineering-grade resins, or urgent rush jobs often command 200% to 400% markup above base cost.
How do I account for failed prints in my pricing?
Adding an 8% to 15% Failure Risk Buffer into every quote ensures that inevitable bed detriments, nozzle clogs, or material imperfections are automatically amortized across your paying jobs.

Understanding Video Export Bitrates & Storage Calculation

Video file size is determined almost entirely by total bitrate and clip duration. Higher resolutions and frame rates require higher bitrates to maintain visual fidelity without compression artifacts like pixelation or color banding.

File Size (Megabytes) = [ Total Bitrate (Mbps) × Duration (seconds) ] / 8

Recommended Bitrate Cheat-Sheet for Creators:

  • YouTube 4K UHD (60 fps): 53–68 Mbps (H.264) or 35–45 Mbps (H.265 / HEVC).
  • YouTube 1080p FHD (30 fps): 8 Mbps (Standard SDR) or 10 Mbps (HDR).
  • Instagram Reels & TikTok (1080p): 10–15 Mbps H.264 to prevent aggressive platform re-encoding compression.
  • ProRes 422 HQ (4K 24 fps): ~880 Mbps (Ideal for master color grading archivals).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my uploaded video quality lower on YouTube/TikTok than my local export?
Platforms automatically re-encode uploaded videos into their own streaming formats (e.g. VP9 or AV1). Uploading with high bitrates ensures the source video retains maximum detail before platform re-compression.
What is the difference between MB and MiB in video sizes?
Decimal Megabytes (MB = 1,000,000 bytes) are standard for video bitrates and hard drive storage specifications, while Binary Mebibytes (MiB = 1,048,576 bytes) are reported by operating systems like Windows.

Understanding TDEE & Body Recomposition Mathematics

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is an estimation of the total calories you burn per 24 hours. Body recomposition—gaining lean muscle while simultaneously shedding fat—requires optimizing your calorie target alongside high protein intake.

Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (Male) = (10 × weight_kg) + (6.25 × height_cm) - (5 × age) + 5 TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier

Why Macro Ratios Matter For Your Fitness Goals:

  • Protein (4 kcal/g): The foundation for muscle protein synthesis (MPS). During calorie deficits, consuming 1.8g–2.4g per kg of bodyweight prevents muscle catabolism.
  • Carbohydrates (4 kcal/g): The primary energy source for high-intensity weightlifting and glycogen replenishment.
  • Dietary Fats (9 kcal/g): Crucial for hormone production (testosterone, thyroid function) and vitamin absorption. Never drop fats below 15–20% of total calories long-term.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time (Recomp)?
Yes! Beginners, returning lifters, or individuals with higher body fat % can successfully build muscle in a slight calorie deficit (-10% to 0%) provided daily protein intake remains high (2.0g+ per kg).
What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?
BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the bare minimum calories your body burns at rest to maintain vital organs. TDEE includes BMR plus physical movement, daily steps, digestion (TEF), and workouts.
